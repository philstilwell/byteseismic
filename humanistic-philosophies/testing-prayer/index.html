Prompt 1: I have proposed a test of prayer below that would not pose any difficulty for any actual God of the universe. Assess whether it would effectively distinguish any actual God from invented Gods.

Would this prayer test actually distinguish a real god from an invented one?

Method The members of a specific religious group would post en mass on social media the following prediction as a result of their prayer to their particular God.

Prediction Starting tomorrow, Mount Fuji will be relocated to a new country every day for a year. This will be preceded by documented prayers to the God in question for this precise miracle.

Validation The fulfillment of this prediction could be validated by citizens and scientists all around the world. The evidence would be far to strong to deny as evidence for the God who had been prayed to for this miracle.

Public and Dramatic The daily relocation of Mount Fuji would be a globally undeniable event, attracting immense attention.

Difficult to Fake Moving a mountain is far beyond human capability, making a hoax unlikely.

Scale Some might argue that even a powerful God wouldn’t perform such a seemingly pointless act.

Open to Interpretation Even with documented prayers, some might attribute the event to an unknown natural phenomenon or another God.

Confirmation Bias This test might only convince those already predisposed to believe in the God who performed the miracle.

Scale An omnipotent God might choose more subtle or meaningful ways to demonstrate power. Moving a mountain, while impressive, could be seen as a showy display compared to something that alleviates suffering or advances humanity’s understanding of the universe.

Open to Interpretation Even with documented prayers, there could be alternate explanations. Perhaps another God intervened, or maybe science just hasn’t discovered the natural phenomenon that caused this.

Confirmation Bias People tend to interpret information in a way that confirms their existing beliefs. Believers in the God who moved the mountain would be convinced, but others might remain unconvinced.

The Public Miracle with Delayed Verification (as discussed previously) This approach offers a more objective test that transcends specific interpretations.

Faith vs. Evidence Some believe faith is independent of evidence, while others seek proof. This test might appeal more to those who value evidence.

The Nature of Miracles The concept of miracles often involves the violation of natural laws. This test would rely on such a violation.

While this test is imaginative, it still faces challenges Natural Explanations: Skeptics might propose natural explanations (e.g., geological shifts, tectonic activity) rather than divine intervention. Interpretation: Some might argue that even if Mount Fuji moved, it doesn’t necessarily prove a specific God’s existence—it could be an unknown force. Multiple Gods: If different religious groups attempt similar tests, and different mountains move, how do we attribute it to a specific God?

Prompt 2: Returning to the prayer test, is there any coherent reason why any actual omnipresent and omnipotent God who wants a personal relationship with each human would not simply make himself known with either miraculously answered prayer or, better yet, a face-to-face interaction?

What changes once we define Analyzing Divine Concealment and the Role of Faith more carefully

Lack of Clarity The hidden man’s actions are confusing and potentially frightening. Similarly, unanswered prayers or ambiguous miracles can leave people questioning God’s intentions.

Indirect Communication If the man wants a relationship, direct communication seems far more effective than notes passed through third parties. Wouldn’t a loving God prefer a personal connection?

Free Will Perhaps God respects human free will and wants faith to be a choice, not a forced belief based on overwhelming evidence. A direct appearance might remove the element of choice.

Mystery and Awe Some argue that a God beyond human comprehension might choose to reveal itself through the beauty and complexity of the universe, fostering a sense of awe and wonder rather than blind obedience.

Gradual Revelation Maybe God reveals itself progressively through personal experiences, historical events, or the lives of religious figures. This allows for individual interpretation and growth in faith.

Traditional View If a personal relationship requires direct communication and constant interaction, then a hidden God remains a paradox.

Alternative View If a personal relationship can involve a sense of connection, love, and purpose even with indirect communication, then a hidden God might be a possibility.

Different Faiths have different perspectives Some religions emphasize a more personal and direct relationship with God, while others focus on following religious teachings and rituals.

Personal Experiences Many people report personal experiences that they interpret as encounters with the divine. These experiences can be powerful and shape their faith.

Free Will and Faith Some theological perspectives argue that God intentionally remains hidden to preserve human free will. If God’s existence were undeniably evident, belief would be automatic, and free choice would be compromised.

Testing and Growth Concealment might serve as a test or a means for spiritual growth. Faith, trust, and seeking are seen as virtues.

Mystery and Awe The hiddenness of God can evoke awe and reverence. Mystery invites exploration and wonder.

Divine Hiddenness Argument Some philosophers argue that the existence of non-believers despite God’s desire for relationship suggests either God doesn’t exist or doesn’t desire such a relationship.

Empirical Evidence Critics argue that if God exists, empirical evidence (e.g., answered prayers, miracles) should be more prevalent.

Inconsistent Revelation Different religious traditions claim varying revelations, leading to confusion.

Suffering and Hiddenness The problem of evil—why a loving God allows suffering—intensifies when God remains hidden.

Theism Some theists find comfort in the mystery, viewing faith as a journey.

Atheism Atheists often see hiddenness as evidence against God’s existence.

  1. Analyzing Divine Concealment and the Role of Faith: The analogy you provide and the question of why an omnipotent, omnipresent God would choose not to make Himself unambiguously known are central to many theological debates about the nature of God and the role of faith.
  2. The Hiddenness of God: One common theological explanation for divine hiddenness is that God remains concealed to preserve human free will and to allow faith to serve as a test of devotion.
  3. Critique of the Concept of Testing Faith: The analogy you use suggests that such a form of hidden love and communication (as in the case of the man behind the curtains) is not only ineffective but also potentially deceptive or manipulative.
  4. Philosophical Arguments Against Divine Hiddenness: From a critical perspective, it may seem incoherent for a deity who desires a personal relationship with individuals to remain hidden and to communicate indirectly.
  5. Theological Defenses: The debate over divine hiddenness and the expectation of faith without direct evidence is deeply complex and varies significantly across different theological and philosophical traditions.
