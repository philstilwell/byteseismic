Commonalities, Differences, and Dependencies

The categories need clarification before comparison. “Not tangible” does not mean nonphysical: emotions have bodily and neural aspects. Abstract objects, if they exist, are a different proposal from mental episodes. “Spiritual” can describe a practice or interpretation without establishing a further realm.

Explanation
  1. Commonalities:
    • All three terms occur in human descriptions and theories; their use does not establish three independently existing realms.
    • They are all subject to various forms of inquiry—scientific, philosophical, or religious.
  2. Differences:
    • Material: physical entities and processes, including things detected only indirectly. Tangibility is not the criterion for being physical.
    • Immaterial: ambiguous between nonphysical entities, abstract objects and intangible descriptions. Thoughts and emotions must not be classified as nonphysical merely because they are not solid objects.
    • Spiritual: may describe religious commitments, a sense of connection or a proposed nonphysical reality. Claims about souls or divine beings require evidence beyond assigning them to a category.
  3. Dependencies:
    • Material: Dependent on physical properties and forces, interacting directly with our senses and providing the foundation for survival and physical well-being.
    • Mental dependence: human thought and emotion depend systematically on brain and bodily activity. That finding does not settle the status of abstract objects such as numbers.
    • Spiritual interpretation: beliefs and practices shape how experiences are described. A proposed external spiritual entity would not be shown to exist—or to depend on belief—by that observation.

Emergence

The immaterial realm is often seen as emergent from complex interactions in the material realm:

  • Complex Systems: Higher-order mental states (like consciousness) emerge from complex neural networks.
  • Emergence: organized systems can have properties absent from isolated components. Weak emergence need not exceed physical explanation; strong emergence claims more and requires separate argument. Merely saying “emerges” does not explain consciousness.
