1Original prompt · edited responses

Prompt 1: Some ideologies emphasize the beauty of mysteries and suggest that the awe generated by the absurd, unfathomable or ineffable constitutes evidence of the ideology’s truth. What dangers does this notion intrinsically hold?

When wonder is made to certify a belief

Claude 2 response · editorial edition

ChatGPT 4 was inaccessible, so Claude 2 was brought in for this discussion.

The intrinsic problem is an unsupported inference: a powerful experience of mystery does not, by itself, distinguish a true ideology from a false one. Two incompatible doctrines may evoke the same awe. Further harms depend on how a community uses that inference; they are risks, not inevitable features of wonder:

  • Rationalizing or justifying beliefs without evidence or logic. If a belief system is deemed true or valid simply because it seems strange or inexplicable, it removes the need for empirical support or reasoned argument.
  • Enabling ungrounded speculation. If every request for a reason is answered with an appeal to mystery, unsupported claims become difficult to challenge. Empirical claims need appropriate tests; philosophical claims still need intelligible arguments, even when experimental falsification is not applicable.
  • Hindering critical thinking and scientific inquiry. Science depends on questioning, testing, and falsifying hypotheses – not mystifying them. An ideology that rejects such inquiry in favor of awe risks impeding understanding.
  • Providing cover for harmful or unethical beliefs. Unfortunately, attributing a belief system’s inscrutability to its truth could be used to shield dangerous, offensive or factually incorrect views from scrutiny.

Wonder can motivate inquiry and remain valuable when no answer is available. The problem is treating the absence of an explanation as support for a particular explanation. Ask what favors that doctrine over an equally mysterious rival, and what could count against it.

Gemini response · editorial edition

Treating awe as evidence creates a gap between the experience and the conclusion. The individual and societal dangers below are possible consequences when that gap is protected from criticism, not outcomes that follow from every encounter with mystery:

Individual dangers:

  • Blind faith: By valuing mystery over critical thinking, individuals might be more susceptible to manipulation and exploitation. They may accept illogical or harmful beliefs simply because they are shrouded in mystery or presented as beyond questioning.
  • Cognitive dissonance: A conflict between a belief and contrary evidence can prompt defensive reasoning, but it can also prompt revision. The concern is a rule that automatically turns counterevidence into another sign of the doctrine’s depth.
  • Isolation and extremism: If a community portrays every outside criticism as proof that outsiders cannot understand its mysteries, members may lose opportunities to correct errors. Isolation or extremism is a further possibility, not an intrinsic result of valuing the ineffable.

Societal dangers:

  • Oppression and intolerance: A clear moral code can still be shielded from criticism by appeals to mystery. The danger is that harmful rules become immune to challenge; mystery does not establish that an ideology lacks moral rules or necessarily produces violence.
  • Stunted progress: By discouraging critical thinking and valuing mystery over reason, such ideologies can hinder scientific progress, social development, and the advancement of knowledge.
  • Authoritarianism: A leader who claims exclusive access to hidden truth can use that claim to evade accountability. The practical questions are whether decisions can be challenged, evidence examined and authority withdrawn—not simply whether the leader speaks of mystery.

It’s important to remember that not all ideologies that acknowledge mystery or grapple with the ineffable fall prey to these dangers. However, it’s crucial to be aware of the potential risks and engage in critical thinking and open dialogue when evaluating any ideology that positions the “absurd” as a source of truth.

The familiar slogan “Credo quia absurdum” is not a verbatim quotation from Tertullian. Its history should not be compressed into a settled claim that he endorsed belief merely because it is irrational. Peter Harrison’s historical study traces the later formulation. Whatever its provenance, a modern appeal to absurdity still needs an argument linking the experience to the proposition asserted.

The central defect is evidential: mystery alone does not establish the doctrine. The social risks become more serious when that reasoning is combined with censorship, coercion or unaccountable authority. Keeping these steps separate makes the criticism more precise.

2Original prompt · edited responses

Prompt 2: How might I respond to someone who encourages me to believe something based on the beauty of its mystery instead of the evidence or argumentation supporting it?

Responding without surrendering the evidence question

ChatGPT response · editorial edition

ChatGPT 4 is back.

When responding to someone who encourages belief based on the beauty of its mystery rather than on evidence or argumentation, it’s essential to navigate the conversation with respect, openness, and critical thinking. Here are several strategies you might consider:

1. Acknowledge the Appeal of Mystery

Start by acknowledging the inherent appeal and emotional resonance of mystery. You might say:

  • “I can appreciate the wonder you find in this. Something can be unexplained without being permanently inexplicable, and neither possibility yet tells us which explanation is true.”

This approach shows that you appreciate the value of mystery in human experience without immediately dismissing their perspective.

2. Express the Importance of Evidence-Based Belief

Next, gently introduce the importance of evidence and rational argumentation in forming beliefs. You could frame your perspective like this:

  • “I want a reason to think this belief is true, beyond the feeling it creates. What evidence or argument favors it over another belief that produces the same sense of mystery?”

This states an evidential requirement rather than presenting reasoning as merely a personal taste. The question is what supports the conclusion.

3. Discuss the Role of Mystery in Knowledge

You can also explore the idea that mystery and evidence are not mutually exclusive but rather parts of a broader pursuit of understanding. Consider saying:

  • “Perhaps there’s a way to appreciate the mystery while also seeking evidence and reasoned arguments. Mystery can inspire us to explore and learn, but evidence helps ground our beliefs in reality.”

This approach suggests a balanced view that values both the inspiration derived from mystery and the clarity provided by evidence.

4. Inquire About the Basis of Their Belief

Engage in a genuine conversation about the foundations of their belief. Ask questions that encourage thoughtful reflection:

  • “I’m curious about what specifically draws you to this belief. Are there aspects of it that you find supported by evidence or compelling arguments, in addition to its mysterious nature?”

Asking questions can foster a deeper dialogue and provide insights into their reasoning process.

5. Highlight the Diversity of Belief Systems

Point out the rich variety of beliefs and ideologies that exist, many of which are surrounded by their own mysteries:

  • “Different traditions can inspire similar awe while making incompatible claims. What would help us decide between them, or decide that we should suspend judgment?”

This response emphasizes the value of exploring multiple perspectives and the criteria we use to understand them.

6. Share Your Comfort with Uncertainty

If applicable, share your comfort with not having all the answers and how this influences your approach to belief:

  • “I’ve come to embrace the fact that some questions don’t have clear answers, and that uncertainty can be a part of the human experience. This acceptance influences how I approach beliefs, leaning more towards those that provide evidence and coherent reasoning, even as I appreciate the beauty of the unknown.”

This personal insight can show how your approach to beliefs is shaped by a broader acceptance of uncertainty and the unknown.

Use the replies that fit the conversation rather than delivering all six as a script. A useful next step is to select one specific claim and agree on what would support or weaken it. Respectful wording helps, but cannot guarantee a productive exchange.

Gemini response · editorial edition

Responding to someone who encourages belief based on mystery rather than evidence requires tact and nuance. Here are some approaches you can consider:

1. Express appreciation for their perspective:

  • Acknowledge their enthusiasm and their appreciation for the mystery. You can say something like, “I understand why you find the mystery beautiful and compelling.”

2. Gently introduce the importance of evidence:

  • You might say, “The beauty helps explain why the idea attracts us. What makes it more likely to be true than an incompatible idea that is equally beautiful?”

3. Ask clarifying questions:

  • Instead of directly contradicting them, ask questions to understand their perspective better. You could inquire, “What makes you see this as evidence supporting the belief?” or “What kind of evidence would change your mind?”

4. Redirect the conversation to specific points:

  • If they mention specific claims, gently ask for supporting evidence or logic behind those claims. You can say, “That’s interesting. Can you elaborate on why you believe that?” or “Do you have any evidence to support this claim?”

5. Share your own sources:

  • If you have researched the topic and have sources with opposing viewpoints or supporting evidence, you can offer to share them respectfully. You can say, “I’ve come across some different perspectives on this, would you be interested in hearing about them?”

6. Emphasize open-mindedness and mutual respect:

  • Respect the person while evaluating the claim: “I take your experience seriously, but I do not yet see how it establishes this conclusion.” Mutual respect does not require treating all claims as equally supported.

7. Set boundaries if necessary:

  • If the discussion becomes uncomfortable or feels manipulative, politely excuse yourself and set boundaries. You can say, “I appreciate the conversation, but I’m not comfortable discussing this further right now.”

The aim is a clear exchange about reasons, not agreement at any cost. Offer to examine one concrete claim together, and leave room for uncertainty or for ending the conversation if it becomes coercive.

Prompt 3: Provide 10 discussion questions relevant to the exchange above.

Two sets of ten discussion questions

ChatGPT response · editorial edition

Creating discussion questions related to the exchange above can foster deeper reflection and conversation on the interplay between mystery, evidence, and belief. Here are ten questions designed to encourage thoughtful dialogue on these themes:

  1. Exploring Mystery and Evidence: How do mystery and evidence-based reasoning contribute differently to our understanding of the world? Can they coexist within a single belief system?
  2. The Value of Mystery: How can the allure of mystery make a belief feel credible without increasing its evidential support?
  3. Evidence and Belief Formation: How important is empirical evidence in forming your personal beliefs? Are there areas in life where evidence plays a less critical role?
  4. The Role of Emotion in Belief: To what extent do emotions influence our acceptance of beliefs that are primarily supported by mystery rather than evidence?
  5. Philosophical Perspectives: How do different philosophical or religious traditions interpret the relationship between mystery and knowledge?
  6. Rationality and Intuition: When might an intuitive impression be useful as a starting hypothesis, and what checks should precede relying on it?
  7. The Limits of Human Understanding: What are the limitations of human understanding and reasoning in comprehending the universe and our existence? Is it possible that some truths are beyond the realm of evidence and argumentation?
  8. Appreciating the Unknown: How can appreciating the unknown and the mysterious enrich our lives or expand our perspectives, even if we prioritize evidence-based beliefs?
  9. Mystery in Science and Religion: How do science and religion differently approach the concept of mystery? Do they see it as something to be solved, embraced, or both?
  10. Dialogue Across Differing Beliefs: What strategies can individuals use to engage in productive and respectful dialogue with others who hold beliefs based on very different foundations (e.g., mystery vs. evidence)?

These questions are designed to encourage exploration of the nuanced relationship between the known and the unknown, the seen and the unseen, and how these realms influence our beliefs and understanding of reality.

Gemini response · editorial edition

  1. Do you believe all mysteries hold inherent beauty? Are there some mysteries we should approach with caution?
  2. What role should evidence play in shaping our beliefs, even when dealing with the ineffable? Can they coexist peacefully?
  3. How can we balance the allure of the unknown with the responsibility to critically evaluate information?
  4. Are there situations where embracing the “absurd” can be beneficial? If so, when and why?
  5. How can we identify and avoid manipulative tactics that exploit the appeal of mystery to promote harmful beliefs?
  6. What are the ethical implications of using “Credo quia absurdum” as a justification for belief?
  7. Can appreciating the mystery of an idea ever be a stepping stone towards deeper understanding and eventual proof?
  8. Should we distinguish a belief’s personal significance from the evidence needed to present it as a publicly established truth? Why or why not?
  9. How can we encourage open dialogue and respect for diverse perspectives, even when dealing with opposing views on the role of mystery in belief?
  10. What tools or resources can we utilize to cultivate critical thinking skills and resist the allure of accepting the “absurd” without verification?

These questions aim to spark further discussion and reflection on the potential dangers and benefits of embracing the “absurd” as a source of truth. The specific prompts you choose to discuss will depend on your individual interests and the context of your conversation.
