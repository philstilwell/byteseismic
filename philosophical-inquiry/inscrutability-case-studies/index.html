Prompt 1: Provide a list of “walls of inscrutability” accompanying particular claims in which all possible outcomes are encompassed by the expansiveness of the claims.

Provide a list of “walls of inscrutability” accompanying particular claims in which all possible outcomes are encompassed by the expansiveness of the claims.

ChatGPT response · editorial edition

Here “walls of inscrutability” names a way of insulating claims from scrutiny, not a technical category covering every religious or mysterious proposition. The examples require different diagnoses: vagueness, inaccessible evidence, flexible reinterpretation and lack of comparative predictions are not identical.

1: Answered Prayer

    • The claim suggests that a deity responds to prayers in various ways, but the outcomes are so broadly defined (including “yes,” “no,” or “wait”) that any event following a prayer can be interpreted as an answer, making the claim unfalsifiable.

    2: Final Judgment

      • Final judgment: an after-death claim may lack presently accessible evidence without being logically compatible with every possible event. Its testability depends on the content and any independently assessable implications. Inaccessibility is distinct from changing a promise after failure.

      3: Divine Protection

        • Divine protection: a specific promise of safety can be contradicted by harm. A wall arises when harm is redescribed as a different kind of protection and no possible outcome is allowed to count against the revised claim.

        4: Lack of Faith

          • Lack of faith: the explanation becomes self-sealing if faith is judged inadequate solely because the promised result failed. An independently specified measure would at least make the condition assessable; failure itself does not establish insincerity.

          5: Cosmic Plan or Destiny

            • The belief that there is a predetermined cosmic plan or destiny for everyone. Under this claim, all events, whether perceived as positive or negative, are part of a grand scheme, making the claim immune to contradiction because every outcome fits within the plan.

            6: Karma

              • Karma: traditions differ. An unspecified connection across unknown lifetimes can resist testing, while particular claims about actions and consequences may be assessable. Do not assume every account requires a supernatural judge or identical reward system.

              7: Synchronicity

                • Synchronicity: a person can find a coincidence meaningful without claiming an unusual causal mechanism. A claim of a statistically unusual pattern needs a defined comparison, selection rule and accounting for how many opportunities there were to notice coincidences.

                8: Placebo Effect

                  • Placebo: placebo effects are researchable and are not inherently inscrutable. Improvement in a placebo group can also reflect natural recovery, regression toward typical symptoms or reporting effects. Calling every unexplained improvement a placebo effect without suitable comparisons is the unsupported move.

                  These claims often serve important functions in various cultural, spiritual, and individual contexts, providing comfort, meaning, and motivation. However, their resistance to empirical scrutiny also makes them subjects of debate and criticism, especially in discussions about the nature of evidence, belief, and knowledge.

                  Research distinction: NIMH placebo workshop distinguishes improvement in a placebo group from effects identified through suitable comparison groups.

                  These examples show ways a claim can become insulated from evidence. Their broad topic alone does not establish that they are untestable or false:

                  1. Answered Prayer:

                  • Expansiveness: This claim doesn’t define how a prayer is “answered.” It could be a direct fulfillment, a redirection to a better outcome, or simply inner peace despite the outcome.

                  2. Final Judgment:

                  • Accessibility: details of an after-death judgment may not be presently observable. That is a different limitation from a claim deliberately reinterpreted to accommodate contradictory outcomes.

                  3. Divine Protection:

                  These “walls of inscrutability” can be frustrating for those seeking concrete answers, but they also serve a purpose. They allow for personal interpretation, faith, and hope, even in the face of uncertainty.

                  The claim that an unfulfilled promise, especially in a spiritual or religious context, is due to a “lack of faith” or a “lack of sincerity” creates a psychological and emotional predicament for sincere believers. This concept can trap individuals in cycles of self-blame and self-disparagement, affecting their spiritual well-being and self-esteem. Here’s an in-depth look at how this dynamic unfolds:

                  Self-Blame

                  1. Internalization of failure: someone taught that sincere faith guarantees a result may blame themselves when it fails. That inference is not warranted by the outcome alone; the promise, its conditions or the proposed causal relationship may instead be mistaken.
                  2. Cycle of Doubt: This dynamic fosters a vicious cycle of doubt and self-questioning. Believers may constantly evaluate and re-evaluate their faith, wondering if it is ever “enough.” This scrutiny can erode confidence in their spiritual beliefs and practices, leading to further self-doubt and even to questioning their worthiness or spiritual value.

                  Self-Disparagement

                  1. Erosion of Self-Esteem: Continuous self-blame can lead to self-disparagement, where individuals view themselves in a persistently negative light. The notion that they are somehow not faithful enough, not sincere enough, or lacking in spiritual dedication can diminish their self-esteem and sense of spiritual identity.
                  2. Spiritual Guilt and Shame: The idea that one’s lack of faith or sincerity is the cause of unmet spiritual promises can imbue individuals with a deep sense of guilt and shame. These feelings are compounded by the belief that they have not only failed themselves but also failed in their relationship with the divine. This can lead to a profound sense of unworthiness and isolation from their spiritual community.

                  Implications for Spiritual Well-being

                  1. Barrier to Spiritual Growth: Instead of fostering a positive and nurturing relationship with their faith, this dynamic can act as a barrier to spiritual growth. The focus shifts from a holistic and compassionate approach to spirituality to a performance-based mindset, where the depth of one’s faith is constantly under scrutiny.
                  2. Disconnection from Community Support: Individuals may feel isolated or alienated from their spiritual community due to their perceived failings. This isolation can deprive them of the support and guidance that could help them navigate their spiritual doubts and struggles.

                  Conclusion

                  The claim that unfulfilled promises are the result of a “lack of faith” or a “lack of sincerity” places an undue burden on sincere believers, leading them into cycles of self-blame and self-disparagement. It overlooks the complexity of faith, the multifaceted nature of spiritual experiences, and the reality that outcomes may not always align with personal desires, regardless of one’s spiritual sincerity or depth of belief. For a healthier spiritual journey, it’s crucial to foster an environment that encourages exploration, questions, and growth without the fear of blame or judgment for the outcomes that are ultimately beyond an individual’s control.

                  Prompt 5: Comment on the increased probability that an ideology heavily employing inscrutability in its foundational claims is false.

                  Comment on the increased probability that an ideology heavily employing inscrutability in its foundational claims is false.

                  ChatGPT response · editorial edition

                  Inscrutability alone does not assign a probability of falsehood. Distinguish an unspecified claim, a difficult test and a history of evasive revisions. The last can count against the reliability of a source when it is more expected under error or deception than under accurate reporting.

                  Challenges in Empirical Testing

                  1. Falsifiability: Popper emphasized possible empirical observations that conflict with a theory. It is an influential account, not a complete agreed definition of science. Probabilistic hypotheses can be evaluated by comparative likelihood even when no finite outcome is logically impossible under them.
                  2. Ambiguity: an underspecified claim may not supply enough content to evaluate. Ask for a stable meaning before attributing a definite probability to it; making a claim precise can reveal both support and contradictions.

                  Psychological and Social Considerations

                  1. Confirmation bias: a flexible interpretation can make contrary events feel supportive. Skeptics can also select evidence. The relevant question is whether the same standards are applied to favorable and unfavorable outcomes.
                  2. Community and Identity: Ideologies serve not just as systems of belief but also as sources of community and identity. The acceptance of inscrutable claims may be influenced more by the desire for belonging and meaning than by empirical truth. This social and psychological context can make the claims more appealing, irrespective of their factual basis.

                  Philosophical Perspectives

                  1. Kinds of assessment: logical, mathematical, normative and empirical claims need different methods. Being outside a simple experiment does not confer truth, nor exempt a claim from coherent argument and criticism.
                  2. Limits of Human Understanding: Some ideologies argue that inscrutability reflects the limits of human understanding and the complexity of the universe. From this perspective, the inability to empirically test certain claims is not necessarily an indicator of falsehood but an acknowledgment of human epistemological limits.

                  Conclusion

                  A stronger conclusion is conditional: if repeated evasions are more likely when a source is unreliable than when it is reliable, they count against that source. They do not automatically disprove every proposition it endorses. Absence of independent support can justify withholding assent without pretending to have calculated a universal falsehood rate.

                  Further reading: Confirmation explains why evidence can change support for a hypothesis without establishing it with certainty.

                  There isn’t a definitive link between inscrutability and falsehood in ideologies. However, there are some reasons why a heavy reliance on vague and unfalsifiable claims can raise concerns about the validity of an ideology:

                  • Lack of Scrutiny: When core tenets are shrouded in mystery, it becomes difficult to critically examine them. This leaves the ideology open to manipulation and misinterpretation.
                  • Confirmation Bias: Inscrutable claims can be easily twisted to fit any situation. This allows believers to find evidence to support their ideology regardless of what actually happens.
                  • Shifting goalposts: document the original criteria and later revisions. An independently motivated correction differs from an ad hoc change made solely to avoid failure. Unfalsifiability and actual evidence of evasive conduct are separate facts.
                  • Appeal to Emotion: Vague claims can be emotionally powerful, appealing to hope, fear, or a sense of belonging. However, this doesn’t necessarily translate to truth.

                  However, it’s important to consider these points as well:

                  • Mystery and Faith: Some ideologies, particularly religious ones, embrace a certain level of mystery. Faith is a core component, and not everything needs a scientific explanation.
                  • Openness to Interpretation: Inscrutability can allow for diverse interpretations and personal connections with the ideology. This can be a source of strength for believers.

                  To infer increased probability of falsehood, specify alternatives and why the observed evasion is more expected under one than another. Merely naming inscrutability supplies no numerical increase. A person can responsibly suspend judgment when the claim lacks enough content or support.

                  Here are some additional factors to consider when evaluating ideologies with inscrutable claims:

                  • Does the ideology make predictions that can be tested?
                  • Does it encourage open dialogue and critical thinking?
                  • Does it promote beneficial conduct? That matters ethically and practically, but beneficial consequences do not establish the factual truth of its foundations.

                  By considering these factors, you can make a more informed judgment about the potential truth value of an ideology, regardless of its level of inscrutability.

                  Prompt 6: If one encounters an ideology that relies heavily on inscrutable claims, what can be asked to possibly make the claims scrutable?

                  If one encounters an ideology that relies heavily on inscrutable claims, what can be asked to possibly make the claims scrutable?

                  ChatGPT response · editorial edition

                  Encountering an ideology that relies heavily on inscrutable claims presents an opportunity for critical engagement and dialogue. To navigate and possibly make these claims more scrutable—or open to understanding and scrutiny—several strategic questions can be posed. These questions are designed to invite clarification, encourage specificity, and promote a deeper examination of the underlying assumptions and evidentiary basis of the claims. Here’s a selection of questions that can be effective:

                  Questions Aimed at Clarification

                  1. Can you define the key terms or concepts more clearly?
                    Asking for precise definitions helps clarify vague or broadly used terms, making the discussion more specific and focused.
                  2. Can you provide specific examples or evidence that support this claim?
                    Requesting concrete examples or evidence encourages the provision of tangible, verifiable information that can be examined.

                  Questions Seeking Specificity

                  1. Under what conditions could this claim be tested or falsified?
                    This question challenges the claimant to consider the empirical testability of their assertions, bringing to light any conditions under which the claims could be objectively evaluated.
                  2. Are there any circumstances under which this claim would not hold true?
                    Identifying exceptions or limitations can help narrow down the scope of the claim, making it more specific and potentially scrutable.

                  Questions Exploring Underlying Assumptions

                  1. What assumptions underlie this claim? Are they justified?
                    Every claim is built on underlying assumptions. Examining these can reveal the foundational beliefs and values, opening them up for scrutiny and discussion.
                  2. How does this claim align with or differ from empirical evidence or established theories?
                    Comparing the claim to existing knowledge and evidence can highlight areas of congruence or discrepancy, prompting a reconsideration of the claim’s basis.

                  Questions Encouraging Reflection and Dialogue

                  1. How would you explain instances where experiences or observations seem to contradict this claim?
                    This question encourages reflection on the claim’s robustness and how discrepancies are reconciled within the ideology.
                  2. Is it possible to interpret the evidence or phenomena in a different way?
                    Encouraging alternative interpretations can open the door to a broader discussion about the claim’s validity and the possibility of different perspectives.

                  Questions About Practical Implications

                  1. What are the practical implications if this claim is true? What if it’s not?
                    Discussing the consequences of the claim’s truth or falsehood can shed light on its significance and the motivations behind its assertion.
                  2. How does this claim influence behavior or decision-making?
                    Understanding the impact of the claim on real-world actions and decisions can reveal its practical utility and ethical dimensions.

                  These questions can improve clarity without guaranteeing a testable answer. Record definitions and expected outcomes before judging results, distinguish logical disproof from probabilistic evidence, and allow suspension of judgment when the claim remains underspecified.

                  Gemini response · editorial edition

                  Here are some questions you can ask to try to make the inscrutable claims of an ideology more scrutable:

                  • Focus on specifics: ask what result, by what date and under what conditions would count for or against the claim. Ask what would be expected without the proposed cause; certainty need not be the standard.
                  • Criteria for Truth: Inquire about the criteria used to judge the truth or falsity of the claims. For example, if the claim is “divine protection keeps us safe,” ask “how do we know when someone is being divinely protected?”
                  • Testable Predictions: See if the ideology makes any predictions that can be tested. This can help assess the validity of the claims. For example, if the claim is “the afterlife exists,” ask “are there any testable predictions about what happens in the afterlife?”
                  • Alternative Explanations: Explore if there are alternative explanations for the phenomena the ideology claims to explain. This can help identify potential biases or weaknesses in the claims.
                  • Historical context: investigate documented origins and revisions. Origins can inform source reliability but do not alone determine whether a proposition is true.
                  • Internal Consistency: See if the claims within the ideology contradict each other. Inconsistencies can raise doubts about the overall coherence of the ideology.

                  Remember:

                  • Approach with respect: ask whether the person wants to discuss the issue. Respect supports fair inquiry but does not guarantee agreement or receptivity.
                  • Use clear questions: open questions can invite explanation, while precise yes/no questions can clarify commitments. Neither format is inherently superior.
                  • Focus on Understanding: The goal is to gain a better understanding of the ideology, not necessarily to win a debate.
                  • Be Prepared for Unanswerable Questions: Some inscrutable claims by nature may not have easy answers. That doesn’t necessarily mean the ideology is false, but it does highlight the limitations of the claims.

                  By asking these types of questions, you can encourage critical thinking and a more nuanced understanding of the ideology’s claims, even if they remain shrouded in a certain degree of mystery.
