Prompt 1: Provide rigorous definitions of core and deep rationality.

Definitions and their scope

(This is based on a discussion in this article.)

ChatGPT response · editorial edition

Core rationality, in the terminology proposed in this conversation, is a disposition of epistemic self-honesty: trying sincerely to align confidence with the evidence as one understands it, including contrary evidence. This is a proposed distinction, not a standard psychological classification. A binary ideal can distinguish honesty from deliberate misrepresentation in a particular judgment; real people vary across situations, and sincere error or unconscious bias does not by itself establish dishonesty.

Deep rationality names acquired critical-thinking tools and relevant knowledge: probability, deduction, induction, methods of inquiry, and recognition of biases and fallacies. These abilities develop by degrees. To make the four combinations requested below coherent, possession of these tools must be distinguished from their honest use: a skilled reasoner can misuse expertise. Core honesty supports good application but is not a prerequisite for acquiring the skills.

The distinction separates an epistemic disposition from a repertoire of skills. Both can be cultivated together. Honest intentions do not guarantee accurate beliefs, and technical sophistication does not guarantee honest reasoning.

Claude response · editorial edition

Core Rationality:

Core rationality here means a sincere disposition to match confidence to perceived relevant evidence and revise it when warranted. The thread proposes a binary ideal for a particular judgment; it does not establish that whole people fall into two empirically measurable classes.

Formally, core rationality can be defined as:

For a proposition P, the ideal is to assign confidence in accordance with one’s honest assessment of the total relevant evidence. No unique numerical mapping follows from this statement alone: a model of evidential support and appropriate background assumptions would also be needed.

Deep Rationality:

Deep rationality refers to acquired skills and knowledge that can improve reasoning. It varies by degree and domain; someone can possess these tools while selectively or dishonestly using them.

Deep rationality involves the development and application of:

  1. Probability theory and formal systems of reasoning
  2. Deductive and inductive tools of science
  3. Awareness and mitigation of cognitive biases and logical fallacies
  4. Relevant knowledge of the world and the ability to distinguish justified probability estimates from unsupported precision.

The key aspects of deep rationality can be defined as follows:

An individual exhibits deep rationality to the degree that they:

  • Accurately understand and apply principles of probability, logic, and scientific reasoning
  • Identify and correct for cognitive biases in their thinking
  • Integrate a comprehensive empirical understanding of the world
  • Evaluate how well confidence tracks evidence, acknowledge uncertainty, and correct identifiable errors.

In summary:

  • Core rationality is the foundational principle of belief/evidence alignment
  • Deep rationality extends core rationality through acquisition of knowledge/skills for more refined rational thinking
  • Core rationality supports honest use of deep rational skills; it is not logically required for possessing those skills.

This taxonomy provides a coherent framework for understanding different levels of rational thought, from the fundamental commitment to aligning beliefs with evidence, to the skilled and continual refinement of rational beliefs and decision-making through specialized knowledge and tools.

Two analogies

ChatGPT response · editorial edition

Certainly! An effective analogy to reflect the concepts of core and deep rationality is the process of building and furnishing a house.

Analogy: Building and Furnishing a House

Core Rationality: Imagine core rationality as the foundation of a house. This foundation must be solid, stable, and well-aligned with the building plans. It doesn’t involve the intricate details of the house’s interior design or architecture; instead, it focuses on ensuring that the house stands firmly and aligns with the basic structural requirements. Just as core rationality involves aligning one’s beliefs strictly with the evidence at hand, the foundation of a house must align with essential stability and level criteria. If the foundation is flawed, the entire structure is at risk, regardless of how well it is decorated or designed.

Deep Rationality: Once the foundation is set, deep rationality is like the advanced architecture and interior design of the house. This involves sophisticated choices about the layout, the materials, the functionalities, and the aesthetics that make the house not only livable but also efficient, beautiful, and uniquely suited to the needs of its residents. Deep rationality encompasses the advanced skills and knowledge — akin to architectural knowledge, interior design, and customization capabilities — that allow for more nuanced and sophisticated decisions that build upon the basic foundation.

Combining Both: A house that stands firm with a strong foundation (core rationality) and is beautifully designed and efficiently laid out (deep rationality) is more complete. It efficiently serves its purpose and satisfies both the basic need for shelter and the higher-level desires for comfort and style. Similarly, an individual who combines core rationality with deep rationality is not only aligned with the evidence (foundation) but also adept at navigating complex problems and making well-informed, nuanced decisions (advanced design).

The house analogy illustrates the importance of combining a sound foundation with capable design. Its limit is that people can acquire advanced reasoning skills before, or without, an honest disposition; the four combinations concern that distinction.

Claude response · editorial edition

Here’s an analogy that tries to capture the relevant aspects of core rationality and deep rationality:

Imagine navigating a forest with a compass. An ordinary compass indicates magnetic north, not the direction of your destination. Core rationality is analogous to honestly taking its reading and other available evidence into account when choosing a direction.

Following a reading sincerely can illustrate core honesty when one has no reason to doubt it. Persisting after recognizing that the compass is faulty would not: honest reasoning must include evidence about the instrument’s reliability.

However, the journey through the forest is complicated by factors like terrain obstacles, forks in the paths, potential to get turned around and disoriented, etc. This is where deep rationality comes in – it provides you with more advanced navigation tools and skills.

Deep rationality would be like having:
1) A detailed topographical map providing awareness of the terrain.
2) Pathfinding algorithms to calculate optimal routes.
3) Techniques to account for compass inaccuracies.
4) Methods to re-orient yourself if you get turned around due to biases.

With these deep rational tools, you can navigate much more effectively – finding better paths, avoiding pitfalls, self-correcting when required, and improving your chances of reaching the destination successfully.

An honest reading provides a starting point, while maps and navigation skills help interpret and check it. Possessing those skills is distinct from choosing to use them honestly.

In this analogy:

  • The compass = Core rationality of aligning beliefs with perceived evidence
  • Advanced navigation tools = Deep rationality skills like probability, logic, bias-awareness
  • Reaching the destination = Successfully applying evidence and skills in this particular case, without a guarantee of success in every case.

The two levels work in synergy, but deep rationality builds instrumental complexity upon the foundational orientation provided by core rationality.

Prompt 4: Provide 3 scenarios that exemplify how core and deep rationality can be used to optimize one’s critical thinking to arrive at superior conclusions or results.

Applied scenarios

ChatGPT response · editorial edition

These three hypothetical scenarios illustrate how honest evidence assessment and suitable reasoning tools can improve decisions. They are examples of a method, not guarantees of a particular clinical, investment, or policy outcome.

Scenario 1: Medical Decision-Making

Context: Dr. Thompson, a pediatrician, faces a challenging case where a child presents with symptoms that could be either a viral or bacterial infection.

Core Rationality: Dr. Thompson uses core rationality to align her initial diagnosis with the evidence presented by the child’s symptoms and medical history. She avoids jumping to conclusions without sufficient evidence, maintaining a commitment to diagnosing based on data rather than assumptions.

Deep Rationality: Employing deep rationality, Dr. Thompson extends her analysis by considering the latest medical research on symptom clusters distinguishing between viral and bacterial infections and uses statistical probabilities to evaluate the likelihood of each diagnosis. She also considers potential biases in symptom reporting and evaluation.

Outcome: A more carefully supported assessment can improve treatment decisions, while uncertainty and diagnostic error remain possible. The example supplies no clinical rule for deciding between particular diagnoses or treatments.

Scenario 2: Investment Strategy

Context: Maria, a financial analyst, is assessing whether to recommend investing in a new tech startup.

Core Rationality: Maria aims to assess the available financial information honestly, including evidence against the investment, and acknowledges uncertainty and potential conflicts of interest.

Deep Rationality: Leveraging deep rationality, Maria applies advanced financial models, considers economic indicators, and evaluates risk using probabilistic scenarios. She also critically assesses her own cognitive biases, such as overconfidence or anchoring, which might sway her analysis.

Outcome: Maria can make the assumptions and risks of her recommendation clearer. Careful reasoning does not guarantee higher returns or prevent losses.

Scenario 3: Environmental Policy Development

Context: An environmental policy maker, Greg, needs to formulate a new policy on water usage that balances agricultural needs with conservation efforts.

Core Rationality: Greg aims to represent evidence about water resources accurately. Choosing a policy also requires explicit judgments about competing interests, costs, and distribution; evidence alone does not settle those values.

Deep Rationality: Utilizing deep rationality, Greg incorporates complex system modeling to predict future water availability under various climate scenarios. He also engages with interdisciplinary knowledge, from hydrology to environmental economics, to understand the broader implications of any policy change.

Outcome: Greg can compare plausible consequences and trade-offs more transparently. Forecast uncertainty, implementation, and competing priorities still limit the result; conservation is not guaranteed.

In each scenario, core rationality supplies an honest stance toward evidence and deep rationality supplies tools for evaluating it. Their combination can improve the quality of reasoning without eliminating uncertainty or settling every value judgment.

Claude response · editorial edition

Yes, that’s an excellent way to summarize the key distinction:

Core Rationality = A disposition of epistemic honesty

Deep Rationality = The acquisition of tools for critical thinking

You’ve captured the essence of it very well. Core rationality is fundamentally about having the sincere intention and commitment to aligning your beliefs with the evidence as you perceive it. It’s the baseline epistemic virtue of intellectual honesty.

Deep rationality, on the other hand, is about purposefully developing the skills, formal methods, background knowledge, and cognitive tools that allow you to engage in more sophisticated and rigorous critical analysis. It provides the arsenal of techniques to effectively scrutinize evidence, identify flaws in reasoning, update beliefs in a maximally truth-tracking way, and arrive at justified, well-calibrated conclusions.

So while core rationality establishes the foundational desire for making evidence-respecting judgments, deep rationality supplies the means for actually executing on that desire with higher fidelity. One is the motive force, the other is the applied expertise.

Your pithy summarization really gets to the heart of how these two facets of rationality interrelate. Core rationality is the epistemic sincerity, and deep rationality is the competent mastery of rational faculties. Developing the latter allows one to better actualize and optimize the former.
