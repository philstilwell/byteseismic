Correlation Between Childhood Trauma/Deprivation and Neurodiversity

“Neurodiversity” describes variation among minds; it is not one condition with one cause. Childhood adversity can affect development and well-being, and neurodivergent children can be more exposed to adversity. These directions and overlapping symptoms must be distinguished.

1. Genetic Predisposition

  • Neurodiversity: Conditions such as autism spectrum disorder (ASD), attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and dyslexia often have a genetic basis. Family history plays a significant role in the likelihood of developing these conditions.
  • Impact of trauma: Trauma can affect functioning and mental health in a person with or without a developmental condition. That does not establish that it caused their autism, ADHD, or dyslexia.

2. Epigenetics

  • Epigenetics: Biological mechanisms affecting gene regulation are studied in stress research. Naming such a mechanism does not demonstrate a causal chain from an experience to a particular neurodevelopmental diagnosis.
  • Evidence needed: A mechanistic claim would require appropriate timing, measures, controls, and replication. The original response supplies no study establishing that trauma-triggered epigenetic changes explain neurodivergence generally.
