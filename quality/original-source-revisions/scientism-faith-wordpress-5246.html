Is there a robust definition of scientism?

Scientism is a term often used to describe the belief that the scientific method, or approaches derived from it, are the most valid or only valid approach to answering questions of reality and gaining knowledge. This belief extends to the idea that scientific knowledge is superior to all other interpretations of life, including philosophical, religious, and traditional forms of knowledge. Critics of scientism argue that it dismisses or devalues non-scientific fields of inquiry and forms of knowledge, failing to acknowledge the limits of science and the value of other forms of understanding.

At its core, scientism holds that:

  • Observable, empirical, testable evidence is the sole or primary source of legitimate knowledge.
  • Science is the only way to understand the world in its entirety, excluding or minimizing the relevance of other disciplines or methods of inquiry.

There are nuanced perspectives within scientism, from strong positions that dismiss any non-scientific knowledge as irrelevant or false, to more moderate stances that simply prioritize scientific knowledge while acknowledging the potential contributions of other fields.

Critiques of scientism often focus on its perceived reductionism, arguing that complex human experiences, values, and ethical considerations cannot be fully understood or resolved through scientific methods alone. They also point to the rich insights provided by the arts, humanities, and social sciences, which explore aspects of human life and the universe not accessible to scientific investigation.

Despite these criticisms, proponents of scientism argue for its foundational role in driving human progress and understanding through rigorous, evidence-based methodologies, highlighting its successes in technological innovation, medical advancements, and our understanding of the natural world.

The term “scientism” gets thrown around a bit, and there isn’t one universally agreed-upon definition. However, here’s a breakdown of the concept to give you a good understanding:

  • Core Idea: Scientism basically refers to an inflated view of science. It can manifest in a few ways:
    • Believing science is the only valid way of knowing anything.
    • Assuming scientific methods can be applied to all aspects of life, even things that might not be well-suited to them (like ethics or aesthetics).
    • Putting science on a pedestal, making it seem beyond criticism.
  • Nuances: It’s important to note that not everyone who emphasizes science is a “scientist” in this negative sense. Science is a powerful tool for understanding the world, and skepticism and evidence-based reasoning are valuable.
  • Criticisms: Critics of scientism argue that it can:
    • Discount other valuable ways of knowing, like philosophy, art, or religious experience.
    • Lead to a dehumanizing view of the world, focusing only on the measurable and material.

So, there’s no single definition, but hopefully this explanation gives you a good grasp of what scientism means and the debate surrounding it.

Some say those promoting scientism claim science can or will explain everything. Is this true or is it a straw-man?

The assertion that proponents of scientism claim science can or will explain everything is often debated and can be seen both as a characterization by critics and as a genuine belief by some adherents. The truth of this assertion depends on the perspective and the definition of scientism being used.

1. Straw-Man Argument

  • Definition: A straw-man argument misrepresents an opponent’s position to make it easier to attack or refute. By exaggerating, misrepresenting, or simplifying the nuances of the opposing view, the argument can appear stronger or more valid.
  • Application to Scientism: Critics of scientism might use the claim that “science can explain everything” as a straw-man because it oversimplifies and exaggerates the beliefs of most scientific advocates. Many who value the scientific method highly still acknowledge the existence of questions that science cannot currently answer or may never be able to resolve fully. These include metaphysical questions, subjective experiences (qualia), and moral values, which are not readily accessible to empirical investigation or may lie completely outside its domain.

2. Genuine Belief

  • On the Other Hand: There are indeed some proponents of scientism who assert a strong form of the doctrine, believing that given enough time and advancement in methodology, science will be able to explain all aspects of the universe, including those currently thought to be beyond its scope. This belief is rooted in the successes of science in expanding our understanding of the world and solving problems that were once thought to be insurmountable.
  • Limitations and Critiques: Even among strong proponents, there is often a nuanced understanding that “explaining everything” does not mean reducing all phenomena to purely scientific terms but rather developing a comprehensive understanding that integrates various forms of knowledge. Critics argue that this perspective underestimates the complexity of certain types of knowledge and experience that may not be fully comprehensible or explainable through scientific means alone.

Conclusion

Whether the claim that “science can or will explain everything” is true or a straw-man largely depends on whom you are discussing. For many, it is a simplification used to critique an uncritical adherence to scientism. For others, it represents an optimistic belief in the power of science to continually expand human understanding. The debate highlights the ongoing dialogue between the capabilities and limits of scientific inquiry and the value of other forms of knowledge.

It can be a bit of both. Here’s why:

This is a straw man in the sense that most scientists and folks who appreciate science wouldn’t claim that. Science itself is constantly evolving, and there may be fundamental aspects of reality that are beyond its reach.
  • Nuance in Scientism: However, some aspects of scientism do downplay the role of other ways of knowing. They might argue that even if science can’t currently explain something, a future scientific explanation is always a possibility. This can come across as dismissive of other disciplines.
  • The Limits of Science: Science is fantastic at explaining the natural world through observation, experimentation, and evidence-based reasoning. But some things, like human consciousness, morality, or the meaning of life, might not be well-suited to scientific inquiry.
