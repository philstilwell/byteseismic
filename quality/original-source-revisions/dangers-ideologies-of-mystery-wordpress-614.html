  • Providing cover for harmful or unethical beliefs. Unfortunately, attributing a belief system’s inscrutability to its truth could be used to shield dangerous, offensive or factually incorrect views from scrutiny.

The notion that the “absurd” or “ineffable” constitutes evidence of an ideology’s truth can indeed hold several dangers, both individual and societal. Here are some key points to consider:

Individual dangers:

  • Blind faith: By valuing mystery over critical thinking, individuals might be more susceptible to manipulation and exploitation. They may accept illogical or harmful beliefs simply because they are shrouded in mystery or presented as beyond questioning.
  • Cognitive dissonance: If an ideology contradicts logical explanations and observable facts, individuals may experience cognitive dissonance, a mental state of discomfort caused by holding conflicting beliefs. They may then resort to mental gymnastics or denial to maintain their faith, potentially harming their emotional and intellectual well-being.
  • Isolation and extremism: When subscribing to an ideology based on the “absurd,” individuals may find it difficult to connect with those who hold different beliefs. This can lead to social isolation and extremism, as they seek out communities that reinforce their views, regardless of how harmful or factually unfounded they may be.

Societal dangers:

  • Oppression and intolerance: Ideologies that embrace the “absurd” and reject reason as a path to truth often lack clear moral frameworks. This can pave the way for discrimination, oppression, and even violence against those who dissent or hold different beliefs.
  • Stunted progress: By discouraging critical thinking and valuing mystery over reason, such ideologies can hinder scientific progress, social development, and the advancement of knowledge.
  • Authoritarianism: Leaders who exploit the allure of the “absurd” can easily manipulate masses by claiming access to hidden truths or divine knowledge. This can lead to authoritarian regimes that repress dissent and stifle individual freedoms.

It’s important to remember that not all ideologies that acknowledge mystery or grapple with the ineffable fall prey to these dangers. However, it’s crucial to be aware of the potential risks and engage in critical thinking and open dialogue when evaluating any ideology that positions the “absurd” as a source of truth.

It’s also worth noting that the original context of “Credo quia absurdum” was quite different from how it’s sometimes interpreted today. It stemmed from the idea that certain religious truths, like the resurrection of Christ, transcend human reason and require faith, not because they are nonsensical, but because they represent a higher order of reality. However, over time, the phrase has been misconstrued to suggest that illogical or nonsensical propositions can be inherently true just because they defy understanding.

Ultimately, the dangers of embracing the “absurd” as a source of truth reside in its potential to hinder critical thinking, fuel extremism, and stifle progress, both for individuals and societies as a whole.
