Formally, it provides a way to update our probability estimates for a hypothesis as we receive new evidence.

9: 1.1.7 In simpler terms, Bayes’ Theorem allows us to update our initial beliefs ((P(A))) about the likelihood of an event (A) happening after considering how likely we are to observe another event (B) in case (A) is true ((P(B|A))), and how likely we are to observe (B) in general ((P(B))). This theorem is particularly useful in cases of uncertainty and has wide-ranging applications, including in fields like machine learning, medical diagnosis, and decision-making processes, where it helps in making inferences based on incomplete or evolving information.
10: 1.2.0 Bayes’ Theorem, also known as Bayes’ rule or Bayes’ law, is a fundamental concept in probability and statistics. It describes how to update your belief about an event (hypothesis) based on new evidence. Here’s a simplified explanation:
11: 1.2.1 Imagine you have an event A, like finding a red marble in a bag. You also have another event B, like picking a marble from the bag. Bayes’ Theorem helps you calculate the probability of event A happening given that you already know event B happened .
12: 1.2.2 Formula:
13: 1.2.3 P(A | B) = (P(B | A) * P(A)) / P(B)
14: 1.2.4 Where:
15: 1.2.5 P(A | B): The probability of event A happening given that event B happened (what we want to find).
16: 1.2.6 P(B | A): The probability of event B happening given that event A happened (how likely evidence B supports hypothesis A).
17: 1.2.7 P(A): The prior probability of event A happening (your initial belief before considering evidence B).
18: 1.2.8 P(B): The probability of event B happening regardless of event A (overall evidence regardless of hypothesis).
19: 1.2.9 Essentially, Bayes’ Theorem takes your initial belief (P(A)), adjusts it based on how well the evidence supports your hypothesis (P(B | A)), and normalizes it considering all possible scenarios (P(B)).
20: 1.2.10 Example:
21: 1.2.11 Imagine you have a bag with 5 red marbles and 5 blue marbles. You pick a marble and it’s red. What’s the probability it’s the specific red marble you had in mind before?
22: 1.2.12 P(A): The probability of picking your specific red marble = 1/10 (1 specific red marble out of 10 total).
23: 1.2.13 P(B): The probability of picking any red marble = 5/10 (5 red marbles out of 10 total).
24: 1.2.14 Now, you want to know P(A | B), the probability of picking your specific red marble given you already picked a red marble.
25: 1.2.15 Using the formula, P(A | B) = (1/5 * 1/10) / (5/10) = 1/10.
26: 1.2.16 This means even though you picked a red marble, your initial guess for a specific red marble remains unchanged, highlighting the importance of prior information and specific evidence.
27: 1.2.17 Bayes’ Theorem has wide applications in various fields like medicine, finance, machine learning, and more. It allows us to continuously update our beliefs and make informed decisions based on new information and evidence.
