Here is my comprehensive critique of the underlying logic in the religious leader’s statements:

Faulty Premise
The central premise that humans would inherently invent a religion aligned with selfishness, individuality and lack of moral accountability is a gross overgeneralization. History shows humans have created religions across the whole spectrum – from hedonistic to highly ascetic. To assert that we would “never” invent a demanding faith like the leader’s is demonstrably false.

Survivorship Bias
The leader employs a form of survivorship bias by pointing to the counterintuitive elements as proof the religion couldn’t be man-made. However, we don’t have a full account of all the failed religions invented by humans, many of which likely had equally peculiar or unpalatable traits that caused them to be abandoned over time. Surviving religions tend to emphasize these distinctive qualities as validity, when in reality it says little about their origins.

Circular Reasoning
There is circular reasoning in declaring the religion divine precisely because it doesn’t comport with human nature, while the very criteria for evaluating “human nature” is shaped by the leader’s religious doctrine itself. It is a self-reinforcing loop of reasoning that lacks an objective, independent framework.

Subjective Interpretation
What qualifies as “austere,” “counterintuitive” or “unpalatable” is highly subjective and culturally relative. Practices considered normal or even liberating in one context could be viewed as extremely harsh by an outsider’s perspective. The leader frames these qualities through their own parochial lens.

Psychological Incentives
Holding a religion as unquestionably divine serves the psychological purposes of the leader – conferring authority, demanding obedience, providing a truth above reproach. There are self-serving incentives for promoting a “humans could never invent this” narrative.

Teleological Argument
Ultimately, the leader constructs a teleological argument – because the religion has characteristics x, y, z, it must have been designed for a divine purpose. This holes-based inductive reasoning can be applied to anything and quickly becomes a circular argument unless met with corroborating evidence.

In summary, while the leader’s arguments may resonate with fervent believers, they collapse under scrutiny. The underlying logic rests on flawed premises, cognitive biases, and a failure to account for subjective cultural interpretations, psychological influences, and a myriad of counter-examples throughout human history that contradict the notion that austere faiths must be divine creations. A more rigorous examination of religious origins is required.
