  2. Lack of objective verification: Since divine revelation is a supernatural and subjective experience, there is no objective way to definitively verify the source or authenticity of any particular religious text or revelation. This leaves room for conflicting claims and interpretations.
  3. Cultural and historical influences: Religious texts and revelations often reflect the cultural, historical, and geographical contexts in which they emerged. This can lead to different religions having scriptures that align with the worldviews and beliefs of their respective societies and times.
  4. Scriptural inconsistencies: There are often inconsistencies, contradictions, or incompatibilities between the core teachings, narratives, and depictions of the divine across different religious scriptures, making it challenging to reconcile them as originating from the same source.
  5. Confirmation bias: Adherents of each religion tend to interpret their own scriptures through the lens of their existing beliefs, finding ways to confirm their validity while dismissing or reinterpreting contradictory elements in other religions’ texts.
  6. Power structures and authority: Religious institutions and authorities have vested interests in maintaining the exclusivity and primacy of their own scriptures, as these texts often form the foundation of their power, influence, and legitimacy.

While this tension may seem paradoxical from an objective standpoint, it persists due to the deeply personal, cultural, and faith-based nature of religious beliefs. Resolving this tension would require either a universally accepted objective verification of a single revelation’s divine origin or a more pluralistic and inclusive approach that acknowledges the potential validity of multiple revelations as diverse expressions of the divine.

Ultimately, the resolution of this tension may depend on whether one views the divine as being capable of communicating through multiple channels and contexts or as being limited to a single, exclusive revelation. This philosophical and theological question remains a subject of ongoing debate and discussion within and across religious traditions.
