Feasibility of Challenging the Notion of a Moral Realm with Moral Anti-Realism

Challenging Social Inertia:
Pushing against the social inertia that upholds the belief in a moral realm is a significant challenge. The notion of a moral realm is deeply embedded in societal norms, traditions, and legal systems. Many people find comfort and guidance in the concept of an objective moral order, making it resistant to change. This entrenched belief is reinforced through education, religion, and cultural narratives that emphasize moral absolutes.

Promoting Moral Anti-Realism:

  1. Educational Initiatives: One of the most effective ways to promote moral anti-realism is through education. By integrating moral philosophy, including moral anti-realism, into educational curricula, students can be exposed to a variety of ethical theories. This can encourage critical thinking and a deeper understanding of the foundations of moral claims.
  2. Public Discourse: Engaging in public discourse through books, articles, debates, and media can help disseminate the ideas of moral anti-realism. Influential thinkers and public intellectuals can play a key role in presenting these ideas to a broader audience.
  3. Scientific and Philosophical Research: Continuing to support and publish research that explores the psychological and neurological bases of moral intuitions can provide empirical support for the anti-realist position. Demonstrating that moral intuitions are grounded in emotional and cognitive processes can help demystify the concept of an objective moral realm.

Replacing “Moral” with “Emotional” or “Socially Acceptable”:
Eliminating the term “moral” from discourse and replacing it with terms like “emotional intuitions” or “socially acceptable behaviors” can clarify the anti-realist position. This linguistic shift emphasizes that what we consider “moral” is often a reflection of collective emotional responses and social conventions rather than objective truths.

Potential Challenges:

  1. Resistance to Change: Many individuals and institutions may resist this shift due to their reliance on traditional moral frameworks.
  2. Misunderstanding and Misrepresentation: There is a risk that the public may misunderstand or misrepresent moral anti-realism, perceiving it as a threat to social cohesion and ethical behavior.
  3. Integration with Existing Norms: Successfully integrating these new terms into existing legal and social frameworks without losing the normative force required for social order and cooperation will be challenging.

Conclusion:
While it is feasible to promote moral anti-realism and encourage a shift in discourse, it requires sustained effort across multiple fronts, including education, public discourse, and scientific research. The process will likely be gradual and met with resistance, but by emphasizing the empirical and rational foundations of moral anti-realism, it is possible to challenge the deep-seated belief in a moral realm and foster a more nuanced understanding of human behavior.

Answer: He uses the example of flipping a switch to stop a train from hitting a child, showing how the moral desirability flows backward from saving the child to pressing the button.

 #4: What is Stilwell’s main argument against the concept of morality?

Answer: Stilwell argues that moral intuitions are actually emotional intuitions and their emergent values, and that the use of the term moral is superfluous.

Answer: Because it can psychologically control others and establish the speaker as “moral” or “good”.

 #6: How does Yudkowsky respond to Stilwell’s objection about normative force?

Answer: Yudkowsky explains that normative force refers to a cognitive algorithm that guides behavior and decision-making, not necessarily an assumption of an actual moral realm.

 #7: What term does Stilwell suggest using instead of “moral landscape”?

Answer: Stilwell suggests using the term “landscape of socially acceptable behaviors”.

 #8: Why does Yudkowsky believe that using terms like “moral” is important?

Answer: Yudkowsky believes it is important because these terms carry significant cognitive and cultural weight that influences human behavior and social norms.

 #9: How does Stilwell view the relationship between moral systems and human emotions?

Answer: Stilwell views moral systems as mappings of human emotions that use terminology to give common emotions greater obligatory force.

 #10: What is Yudkowsky’s stance on the objectivity of morality?

Answer: Yudkowsky argues that moral judgments are subjunctively objective and can be true based on a stable, abstract computation of what is right.

 #11: According to Yudkowsky, what is the role of cognitive algorithms in moral decision-making?

Answer: Cognitive algorithms guide human behavior and decision-making by labeling certain actions as “right” or “should”.

 #12: What does Stilwell suggest about the legitimacy of moral systems?

Answer: Stilwell suggests that moral systems lack legitimate grounding and are essentially extensions of emotional dispositions.

 #13: How does Yudkowsky propose to address moral anti-realism’s critique of morality?

Answer: Yudkowsky proposes understanding morality as a complex, abstract computation that guides behavior and decision-making, rather than as an external, objective realm.

 #14: What is the significance of “The Gift We Give To Tomorrow” in Yudkowsky’s argument?

Answer: It signifies that, despite the evolutionary origins of morality, the values and behaviors that promote human flourishing are worth preserving and advancing.

 #15: What challenge does Yudkowsky identify in reflecting on one’s moral intuitions?

Answer: The challenge is to use one’s current best understanding of morality to critically evaluate and possibly revise one’s own moral intuitions.
