Provide rigorous definitions of core and deep rationality.

(This is based on a discussion in this article.)

Core Rationality:
Core rationality is defined as the fundamental level of rationality that encompasses epistemic self-honesty. It is predicated on the alignment of one’s degree of belief with the degree of available evidence pertaining to a given proposition. This form of rationality asserts that rational belief directly corresponds to the perceived balance of evidence, both confirming and disconfirming. Core rationality is therefore binary; an individual either exhibits this alignment or they do not. The absence of this alignment indicates core irrationality. This concept emphasizes the basic commitment to intellectual integrity by adjusting one’s confidence in beliefs based solely on the evidence at hand.

Deep Rationality:
Deep rationality builds upon the foundation of core rationality and involves a more nuanced engagement with rational thought through the acquisition of advanced skills and knowledge. This form of rationality includes a mastery of probability theory, a profound understanding of both deductive and inductive reasoning, a keen awareness of logical fallacies and cognitive biases, and a well-rounded comprehension of the physical world and its inherent probabilities. Deep rationality is not binary but exists on a continuum, as the skills and knowledge that characterize it are typically developed over time and with deliberate effort. It represents a higher, more sophisticated level of rational engagement that enhances the core rational commitment to evidence-based belief formation.

In the proposed taxonomy, core rationality serves as the accessible, universal base of rational thought, emphasizing the honest appraisal and alignment of belief with evidence. Deep rationality, in contrast, represents a graduated development, offering tools and skills that refine and expand one’s ability to engage in complex and nuanced rational deliberations. Together, these definitions provide a comprehensive framework for understanding rationality in a layered and progressive manner, useful for both educational purposes and personal intellectual development.

Core Rationality:

Core rationality refers to the foundational principle of aligning one’s degree of belief (or confidence) with the degree of perceived relevant evidence. It is a binary state – for any given belief, one either exhibits core rationality by mapping their confidence to the evidence, or fails to do so, exhibiting irrationality.

Formally, core rationality can be defined as:

For any proposition P, an individual exhibits core rationality with respect to P if and only if their degree of belief/confidence in P matches the degree to which the perceived evidence supports P.

Deep Rationality:

Deep rationality builds upon core rationality by incorporating additional skills and knowledge that allow for more refined and well-calibrated rational thinking. It exists on a spectrum rather than a binary state.

Deep rationality involves the development and application of:

  1. Probability theory and formal systems of reasoning
  2. Deductive and inductive tools of science
  3. Awareness and mitigation of cognitive biases and logical fallacies
  4. Deep understanding of objective reality and its intrinsic probabilities

The key aspects of deep rationality can be defined as follows:

An individual exhibits deep rationality to the degree that they:

  • Accurately understand and apply principles of probability, logic, and scientific reasoning
  • Identify and correct for cognitive biases in their thinking
  • Integrate a comprehensive empirical understanding of the world
  • Arrive at belief calibrations that closely track the true evidential support and objective reality

In summary:

  • Core rationality is the foundational principle of belief/evidence alignment
  • Deep rationality extends core rationality through acquisition of knowledge/skills for more refined rational thinking
  • Core rationality is a prerequisite for deep rationality, which builds upon it incrementally

This taxonomy provides a coherent framework for understanding different levels of rational thought, from the fundamental commitment to aligning beliefs with evidence, to the skilled and continual refinement of rational beliefs and decision-making through specialized knowledge and tools.
