3 1.1.1 Local Cost of Living Adjustments :
4 1.1.2 Living wages are typically calculated based on the cost of living in a specific area. This includes housing, food, healthcare, education, and other necessities. Various organizations conduct detailed surveys to determine these costs and set a living wage that would allow a worker to meet their basic needs and that of their family.
5 1.1.3 Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) :
6 1.1.4 This is an economic theory that compares different countries’ currencies through a “basket of goods” approach. By adjusting for PPP, one can gauge what the same amount of money can buy in different countries, providing a more level playing field for measuring wage adequacy.
7 1.1.5 Global Living Wage Coalition (GLWC) :
8 1.1.6 The GLWC aims to develop internationally comparable estimates. They work with local stakeholders and international researchers to establish benchmarks that reflect both the local cost of living and basic but decent living standards. These benchmarks are crucial for multinational companies operating in different countries.
9 1.1.7 International Labour Organization (ILO) :
10 1.1.8 The ILO provides guidelines and support for establishing a living wage and includes calculations based on needs (food, housing, etc.) and ability to pay. They often work towards setting standards that influence policy-making at national and international levels.
11 1.1.9 World Bank and United Nations :
12 1.1.10 These international bodies also provide data and support for wage analysis, focusing on poverty thresholds and basic living standards which indirectly inform living wage calculations.
13 1.1.11 Each of these methods and organizations emphasizes the adaptability and relevance to local conditions when determining a living wage. While there’s no singular global standard due to the inherently local nature of living costs, these efforts help provide frameworks that can be adapted universally, promoting fair wages worldwide.

Here’s how this is structured and why migration costs are generally excluded:
46 3.1.1 Purpose of Living Wage Calculations
47 3.1.2 Local Sustainability :
48 3.1.3 A living wage calculation aims to ensure that workers earn enough to afford a decent standard of living in their current location. This includes costs like housing, food, healthcare, education, transportation, and other necessities based on local prices.
49 3.1.4 Stability Rather Than Mobility :
50 3.1.5 The focus is on enabling individuals and families to live adequately where they are currently located rather than facilitating movement to new areas. It’s about providing a sustainable living condition in one’s current environment.
51 3.1.6 Why Migration Costs Are Excluded
52 3.1.7 Specificity to Location :
53 3.1.8 Living wages are calculated specifically for a given location or region and are based on the cost structure of that particular area. Including the cost of migration would require assumptions about where people might move from or to, complicating the calculation with too many variables.
54 3.1.9 Variability and Scope :
55 3.1.10 Migration costs can vary widely depending on the distance, the number of family members involved, the reasons for moving (e.g., for a job, for safety), and the difference in cost of living between the origin and destination. This variability makes it impractical to incorporate into a standard living wage calculation.
56 3.1.11 Purpose of Calculation :
57 3.1.12 The primary goal of a living wage is to ensure that workers in a specific location can earn sufficiently to meet basic standards of living without needing secondary income sources. It is not intended to cover transitional costs like relocation unless the job specifically requires frequent moves (e.g., military personnel).
58 3.1.13 Contexts Where Migration Might Be Considered
59 3.1.14 Displacement and Resettlement Programs :
60 3.1.15 For situations involving large-scale displacements (due to environmental issues, conflict, or development projects), specific resettlement allowances might be calculated. These are distinct from standard living wage calculations and are usually part of humanitarian aid or government resettlement packages.
61 3.1.16 Corporate Relocation Policies :
62 3.1.17 Companies that require employees to move frequently or relocate for work typically offer separate relocation packages that cover these costs, which are not included in the living wage but are negotiated as part of employment terms.
63 3.1.18 In conclusion, while the cost of migrating to a new region is a significant consideration for many workers, especially in global or national contexts involving significant economic disparities, these costs are not included in living wage calculations. These calculations are focused on ensuring a decent standard of living within a specific locale based on its cost structure.
